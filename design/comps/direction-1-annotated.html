Case study / fork in the road

Rebuilding Fork In The Road: 113,000 lines became 18,000 that do more

Fork In The Road helps groups decide where to eat without a group chat meltdown. Version one worked but had grown to 113k lines, 27 runtime dependencies, and 77 API routes that nobody could hold in their head. The constraint for version two: keep every shipped feature, cut everything else, and let guests vote securely without accounts.

roleSolo: design, build, ship
timeline9 phases, one PR each
statusLive at forkintheroad.app

Decision: sign the ballot, not the guest

Guest voting is an unauthenticated write surface, which is normally a security hole. Instead of forcing accounts, each guest gets an HMAC-signed httpOnly cookie holding zero personal data. A revote replaces the ballot in place, and a guest who later signs up claims their history through a one-way pointer.

Tradeoff taken: a guest who clears cookies loses their vote. Acceptable, because the alternative was an account wall that kills group adoption.

Decision: kill the cron

Rounds need to expire on time, but cron jobs fail silently and cost money at idle. Expiry became lazy: any read seals an expired round, and the SSE stream that pushes live results doubles as the timer. The seal is crash-safe because it is derived from timestamps, not from a job having run.

Tradeoff taken: a round nobody reads seals late. Nobody is waiting on a round nobody reads.

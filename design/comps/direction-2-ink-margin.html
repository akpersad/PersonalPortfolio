Case study no. 01

Rebuilding Fork In The Road: 113,000 lines became 18,000 that do more

Role: solo, design through deploy Shape: 9 phases, one PR each Live: forkintheroad.app

Fork In The Road helps groups decide where to eat without a group chat meltdown. Version one worked, but it had grown to 113k lines, 27 runtime dependencies, and 77 API routes that nobody could hold in their head. The constraint for version two: keep every shipped feature, cut everything else, and let guests vote securely without creating accounts.

Margin note. The decision math was ported as pure functions with bit-identical parity tests against v1, so the rewrite could never quietly change a result.

Decision: sign the ballot, not the guest

Guest voting is an unauthenticated write surface, which is normally a security hole. Each guest gets an HMAC-signed httpOnly cookie holding zero personal data; a revote replaces the ballot in place, and a guest who signs up later claims their history through a one-way pointer. The tradeoff taken: clear your cookies, lose your vote. Acceptable, because the alternative was an account wall that kills group adoption.

Decision: kill the cron

Rounds must expire on time, but cron jobs fail silently and cost money at idle. Expiry became lazy: any read seals an expired round, and the SSE stream pushing live results doubles as the timer. The tradeoff: a round nobody reads seals late, and nobody is waiting on a round nobody reads.

Margin note. The dark mode axe scan caught white-on-gold at 1.3:1 and forced a mode-invariant gold-ink token. Tooling is evidence; the token is the fix.
